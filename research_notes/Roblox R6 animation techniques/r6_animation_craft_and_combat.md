## 1. What makes R6 animation look good, what the "R6 style" is, and how animators make up for having no elbows or knees

### Takeaway
R6 has six parts: a head, torso, two arms and two legs. There are no elbows, knees or waist, and the torso is driven by the single RootJoint. Animators make up for this by **exaggerating poses well past anatomy**, using **whole-body motion** (torso bob, lean and twist on the RootJoint, counter-rotating the hips so the feet stay planted), **snappy timing** (slow in, fast out), **arcs**, and, for the popular anime/battlegrounds look, **few strong keys with Constant/"None" stepped interpolation or a low-FPS look**. The R6 combat look is "snappy": fast acceleration and deceleration, few keyframes, exaggerated key poses.

### Cited Findings
- **Exaggerate because the rig has fewer joints than a human.** "Try to exaggerate each of the character's movement, for rigs with less joint than the amount of joints a human has, exaggerating the movement helps the character feel more alive." The same answer adds: "The best way to exaggerate motion is by starting their movement slowly, then ending it off fast." [Anecdotal, but it matches combat advice elsewhere] — [DevForum: Advice for positioning R6 limbs while animating?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advice-for-positoning-r6-limbs-while-animating/1533251)
- **Simulating elbows and knees:** one thread suggests treating R6 limb pivots as if the joints sat at the hip/shoulder and refining from there, "a simplification that gets the job done when starting to animate R6 characters." [Anecdotal] — [DevForum: Advice for positioning R6 limbs](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advice-for-positoning-r6-limbs-while-animating/1533251)
- **R6 torso mechanics (technical, important):** the R6 torso is driven by one motor (RootJoint), and the legs hang from the torso. Rotating the torso therefore rotates the legs too. One developer's torso-bend solution: "changing the rotation Torso motor changes the rotation of the legs. To counteract this I have added an inverse rotation so it works." In practice, when you lean or twist the torso, counter-rotate the Left/Right Hip motors to keep the feet planted. — [DevForum: Bending player torso on an R6 rig](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/bending-player-torso-on-an-r6-rig/1945245)
- **Compared with R15:** R15 has real elbow, knee and waist joints. "A bent elbow, a torso lean, or a head tilt are all impossible on R6 without hacky workarounds." (A vendor blog. In practice a torso lean on R6 is done through RootJoint, as above.) — [Kitsblox: R6 vs R15 Rigs 2026](https://kitsblox.com/blog/r6-vs-r15-roblox-rigs)
- **Rig shape dictates movement (official):** Roblox's own animation tutorial says the rig is the first design decision. A blocky character "with mass spread through the body and limited range of motion might shuffle with a short stride," while an articulated Rthro rig with mass near the hips can sway with a longer stride. [Official] — [Roblox Creator Docs: Create character animations](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/animation/create-an-animation)
- **Torso bob adds life:** in feedback threads on stiff R6 walks, the most common fix is "the torso could bob up and down a little more," plus applying easing (in Moon Animator, select keys, press 7, pick an ease style, and try the types). [Widely repeated in feedback threads] — [DevForum: Tips for making r6 animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tips-for-making-r6-animations/3679871); [DevForum: Feedback on my R6 Animation (First Time)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-r6-animation-first-time/3192280)
- **Walk-cycle readability on R6:** lifting legs above the waist "looks very weird compared with a normal swinging motion." Keep legs closer under the body, because legs that "jut extremely far out" read as a crouch. Do not swing the same-side arm and leg together ("looks very unnatural"). [Anecdotal, consistent across threads] — [DevForum: Alternative R6 Animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/alternative-r6-animations/1684667); [DevForum: Feedback on 2nd R6 Walk Animation](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-2nd-r6-walk-animation/1779706)
- **The snappy / retro combat look:** "snappy animations typically have fast acceleration and deceleration, making them feel more impactful." Use limited animation, meaning fewer keyframes with exaggerated poses. [Anecdotal, consistent with other combat threads] — [DevForum: Fist Retro Style Animation?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/fist-retro-style-animation/2419921)
- **Low-FPS / blocky style recipe:** set the editor to 30 fps, add keyframes, and set easing to None (Moon Animator) or Constant (Roblox Animation Editor). "The less key frames you add, the more blocky it will be." A reply suggests simply setting Constant on all keys. A code alternative renders characters at a lower framerate (for example 11 fps) through a LocalScript for a "Spider-verse type feel." Caveat: Constant easing on keys does not lower the framerate of blends *between* animations. — [DevForum: How to make Low FPS / Blocky Style Animation](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-low-fps-blocky-style-animation/2800541); [DevForum: Render Characters At Lower Framerates](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/render-characters-at-lower-framerates/2985668); [DevForum: How to replicate low-fps animation style](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-replicate-low-fps-animation-style/1994174)
- **Arcs:** Jespone's guide (a long-running DevForum community tutorial on the 12 principles) says that if a character's movement follows arcs the animation is working, and without them the character looks like a robot. On overlapping action: apply it to any body part "though not usually the root," and don't overdo it, because "too much flow can be a lot for a rig unless you want a very cartoony look." [Widely referenced tutorial] — [DevForum: Jespone's guide to animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752)
- **Snappier combat tolerates odd limbs:** in a game-animation review, "snappier combat can get away with some oddities with limbs and often even be enhanced by them." But "snapping limbs is something that the eye catches quite easily" in transitions. [Anecdotal, game-jam review] — [itch.io jam rating comment](https://itch.io/jam/sfasx-game-animation/rate/512055)

### Inferences
- The "R6 style" in combat is in effect *limited animation*, as in 2D anime and fighting games. Strong, exaggerated key poses carry the read, with very little in-between. That is why fighting-game advice (Skullgirls/Cartwright, section 2) transfers well to R6.
- Without elbows, a punch's extension has to come from the **torso twist (RootJoint yaw) plus shoulder rotation plus a forward root/torso translation**, not from the arm straightening. The arm is always "straight", so reach and power read through body rotation and lean.
- Because the torso rotation drags the legs, any skill or prompt that writes R6 keyframes should treat "torso lean/twist + inverse hip counter-rotation" as a single operation.

- No source found quantifying "how far" to exaggerate (for example degrees of torso twist on a punch).

## 2. Combat animation structure (windup/active/recovery, M1 combos, dashes, blocks, stuns, knockback, ultimates) and how battlegrounds games make hits feel impactful

### Takeaway
The battlegrounds standard (The Strongest Battlegrounds and imitators) is a **4-hit M1 string**. Hits 1–3 apply light hitstun (about **0.2 s** reported) that holds the target. Hit 4 is a heavy **knockback or downslam** that usually ragdolls. Most systems add an **uptilt** and a **downslam** variant. Impact comes from: **a strong windup of a couple of frames** → **a very fast strike that overextends** → **an abrupt stop on contact** → **a slower, effortful recovery**. Add **hit-stop** (about 2–4 frames, or about 0.05 s light / 0.09 s heavy, reported), **camera shake**, **impact frames** for heavy hits, **sound**, and **VFX/hitboxes fired from animation markers** on the exact contact frame.

### Cited Findings
**M1 combo design**
- TSB-style string: "Every character features a 4-hit M1 attack string. The first 3 hits inflict light hitstun (0.2s), holding the opponent stationary. The 4th M1 hit delivers a heavy Knockback or Downslam, throwing the enemy away and ending the string." [Community wiki/guide, not official] — [games.gg TSB combat guide](https://games.gg/roblox/guides/the-strongest-battlegrounds-combat-guide/); see also [TSB Wiki: Basic Combat](https://the-strongest-battlegrounds-rblx.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Combat)
- Every character's M1 combo strikes 4 times, and the final hit deals the most damage. — [Sportskeeda: TSB beginner's guide](https://sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/the-strongest-battlegrounds-a-beginner-s-guide)
- Ragdoll is "typically applied at the end of most attacks and basic M1 combos." A knocked-down player cannot act for about **2 seconds**. Ragdoll Cancel is reportedly available once per 30 s, and "you cannot dash in m1 stun." [Community wiki] — [TSB Wiki: Techniques](https://the-strongest-battlegrounds-rblx.fandom.com/wiki/Techniques); [TSB Wiki: Basic Combat](https://the-strongest-battlegrounds-rblx.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Combat)
- A common battlegrounds layout per a DevForum developer: "4-5 M1's, a down slam and an uptilt," plus a combo reset on a timer. — [DevForum: Logic Gate For Combat System](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/logic-gate-for-combat-system/3101051)
- **Chain the M1s so each ends where the next begins.** One commercial M1 set ships "1 set of m1s (chained, so they will flow better)" alongside unchained versions. An itch.io R6 combat pack has every move return to a shared fighting stance so moves link. — [Gumroad: Roblox Combat M1 animation set](https://citrusapples.gumroad.com/l/RobloxCombatSet); [itch.io: Combat Animation Pack (R6) by wollnie](https://wollnie.itch.io/combat-animation-pack-r6)
- Commercial R6 packs aimed at battlegrounds games advertise **hit markers on every attack** for damage, sounds and VFX. Typical scope: fist, dagger, sword, spear and greatsword M1s, plus aerial and crit variants per weapon. — [BuiltByBit: R6 Combat Animation Pack](https://builtbybit.com/resources/r6-combat-animation-pack.129091/); [BuiltByBit: R6 Anime Animations Pack](https://builtbybit.com/resources/r6-anime-animations-pack.130971/); [BuiltByBit: R6 Weapon Combat Animations](https://builtbybit.com/resources/r6-weapon-combat-animations.112425/updates)
- Scripting hygiene for M1 chains: "always stop any currently playing M1 before starting a new one" so overlapping tracks don't blend into mush. — [DevForum: How to smoothly transition between M1 animations?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-smoothly-transition-between-m1-animations/3639185)

**Making strikes feel powerful (shape of the motion)**
- In "Combat Animation Lacks Power," replies drawing on Mariel Cartwright's GDC talk recommend: **overemphasised keyframes**; a frame where the attack **overextends then retracts**; **"a couple of frames of very emphasised windup"**; **skipping frames or using smudges (smears)**; and trying **Back and Elastic** easing, which the replier found "more powerful." Another reply: the character "should feel like it has to recover from using great force." The OP's punch read as "a light tap" because there was no effort in the recovery. Sound effects also add strength. [Anecdotal forum replies citing a GDC talk] — [DevForum: Combat Animation Lacks Power](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/combat-animation-lacks-power/1875328)
- **Fast swing, abrupt stop, slow retract.** Too much interpolation weakens impact. [Anecdotal] — [DevForum: How would I go about making my combat feel more impactful?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-would-i-go-about-making-my-combat-feel-more-impactful/2042214); [DevForum: Help with Combat](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-combat/3844625)
- **Skullgirls (transferable):** the team often conveys a quick, strong attack by **snapping straight to the active pose**, then letting hair and clothing follow through. The player can cancel after the key pose, so the animation never blocks gameplay. — [Skullgirls dev blog: Animation: Getting the Details Right (2011)](https://skullgirls.com/2011/05/animation-getting-the-details-right/)
- **Cartwright GDC (transferable):** even **one frame of anticipation** adds contrast and makes a move read stronger. One frame can **overshoot** the final attack pose before settling. Inbetweens should **emphasise the keys** when frames are limited. Smears bridge large movements. Clear silhouettes in key poses are the base of readability. Vary how long each frame is held rather than holding every frame equally. Avoid over-animation: she cites a 21-frame knee-fall as too long. The team used per-move frame-count guidelines for weak, medium and heavy attacks. — [GDC slides: Powerful and Effective Animation for 2D/3D Games (Cartwright)](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcchina14/presentations/833784_MarielCartwright_PowerfulAndEffective_EN.pdf); [GDC Vault page](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021657/Powerful-and-Effective-Animation-for); [Game Anim summary](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/01/26/making-fluid-and-powerful-animations-for-skullgirls/)
- Typical pose keys for an attack: "Key the 5 major poses": **anticipation, wind-up, contact, follow-through, recovery**. Let easing fill in between. [Anecdotal, commercial blog] — [Kitsblox: Why Your Roblox Animations Look Choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)

**Hit-stop, camera shake, impact frames, markers**
- Hit pause: "You could try using hit pauses after the player hits the other player to make the hit feel stronger and more impactful … for strong attacks, adding impact frames would make them feel heavier." [Anecdotal forum reply] — [DevForum: Help with Combat](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-combat/3844625)
- Hit-stop magnitude: "freezing both characters for **2-4 frames** on heavy hits," described as a major juice technique. [Commercial blog] — [Kitsblox: How to Make a Combat System in Roblox (2026)](https://kitsblox.com/blog/how-to-make-combat-system-roblox); [Kitsblox: Best Roblox Combat Animations](https://kitsblox.com/blog/best-roblox-combat-animations). A non-Roblox (Godot) write-up uses about **0.05 s** for normal hits and **0.09 s** for finishers, with a timer that must ignore time scale. [Transferable] — [DEV Community: Improving Combat Feel with Hit Stop…](https://dev.to/hirodeath/improving-combat-feel-with-hit-stop-slash-effects-and-combo-animations-3dbo)
- Impact frames: hold "for no longer than **1 frame at 24 fps**," and save them for important hits rather than every strike. [Anecdotal, TikTok creator] — [TikTok: How to Add Impact Frames on Moon Animator](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-add-impact-frames-on-moon-animator). Hand-drawn impact frames in Roblox are typically done with particle/flipbook approaches. — [DevForum: How Could I Implement Drawn Impact Frames?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-could-i-implement-drawn-impact-frames/2591414)
- **Fire hitboxes, VFX, SFX, hit-stop and camera shake from animation markers** (for example a "HitFrame" marker via `AnimationTrack:GetMarkerReachedSignal()`) so "the moment of damage should match the visible impact." A keyframe's *name* is not a marker. [Widely repeated] — [DevForum: Help with AnimationEvents for Combat System](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-animationevents-for-combat-system/3244308); [roblox-vfx-animation skill doc](https://skillsmp.com/creators/adnvli/vfxskill/skill); [Classic R6 Combat System video, which calls "setting the animation event" the most important part](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fq_5V5tm_t0)
- Combat blends: a **0.1 s** fade between attacks is "fast enough that players will not notice the blend but slow enough to eliminate visual snapping." [Commercial blog] — [Kitsblox: Why Your Roblox Animations Look Choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)
- A missing **block** animation makes combat feel lacklustre. [Anecdotal] — [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/7362765)

**Dashes / root motion**
- Roblox has **no built-in root motion**. Older experienced replies say the HumanoidRootPart is not meant to be moved by an animation: "if you want something to move while an animation plays, you're going to have to script the movement." Workarounds: script the dash (velocity / LinearVelocity / CFrame tween on HRP) while the limb animation plays, or preprocess (extract root translation curves, strip root translation and Y rotation from the KeyframeSequence, then drive the HRP from the curves), or use the community RootMotion module (R6 and R15). — [DevForum: Help with Root Motion](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-root-motion-animating-the-humanoidrootpart/1556616); [DevForum: R6 humanoidrootpart animate](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-humanoidrootpart-animate/1828088); [DevForum: RootMotion module](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/rootmotion-module-animate-the-humanoidrootpart-with-ease-simple-no-conversion-needed/3843087)

**Ultimates / cinematic moves**
- Moon Animator treats the camera as a keyframeable object. Exported camera values are offsets from a reference part if one was set, otherwise world positions. Play camera tracks frame by frame on RenderStepped, not `task.wait`. For cutscenes, set the camera to Scriptable and temporarily anchor the HRP. Moon's keyframe → fill "wiggle" can stand in for camera shake. Anchor rig RootParts in the scene or characters may fly off. [Anecdotal forum threads, menus may have changed] — [DevForum: Moon Animator 2 Camera Animation Scripting](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/moon-animator-2-camera-animation-scripting/2427795); [DevForum: Camera Animations for cutscenes and gameplay](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/camera-animations-for-cutscenes-and-gameplay/1181691?page=2); [DevForum: Help with Moon Animator Cutscene](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-moon-animator-cutscene-please/3754636); [DevForum: Play Moon Animator Camera Cutscene In-Game](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/play-moon-animator-camera-cutscene-in-game/1830691)

### Inferences
- Unit conversion for the skill: 0.2 s of hitstun = **12 frames at 60 fps / 6 frames at 30 fps**. 2–4 hit-stop frames at 60 fps = about 0.033–0.067 s, which matches the 0.05 s light / 0.09 s heavy Godot numbers. A 0.1 s blend = 6 frames at 60 fps.
- A defensible M1 template from the sources: anticipation (1–3 frames, very emphasised) → strike (very fast; snap or 1–2 frames; overshoot by 1 frame) → contact (marker; hit-stop) → recovery (slower, showing effort, ending in a shared fighting stance so hit N+1 chains). Alternate arms across hits 1–3. Hit 4 uses a bigger body commitment (full torso twist, step-in or jump) for knockback/downslam. The alternating-arms pattern is how commercial packs are structured, but I found no source stating it as a rule. See Gaps.
- Hit reacts should be fast and readable (snap into the hit pose and hold during hit-stop), and the 4th-hit knockback should hand off to ragdoll or physics rather than be fully keyed.

- No developer statement or teardown found for TSB's actual camera shake, hit-stop or VFX timing. Community wikis cover only mechanics (stun, ragdoll).

## 3. Readability vs responsiveness in PvP: how short startup can be and still read; telegraphing

### Takeaway
The best-sourced principle comes from fighting-game animation (Cartwright/Skullgirls), which Roblox animators cite directly. **Player attacks need fast startup, but even one frame of anticipation adds readable contrast.** Enemy/NPC attacks need more anticipation so players can react. Roblox forum advice says "a couple of frames of very emphasised windup." Readability comes from **pose contrast and silhouette, not duration**.

### Cited Findings
- "Even one frame of anticipation is enough," and "player characters generally need to move quicker than enemies to be responsive." Enemy attacks need more anticipation so players have time to react. [Transferable, primary GDC slides] — [Cartwright GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcchina14/presentations/833784_MarielCartwright_PowerfulAndEffective_EN.pdf)
- Skullgirls snaps to the active pose for quick, strong attacks. Follow-through (hair, cloth) sells the motion after the hit, and the move is cancellable after the key pose, so it never blocks gameplay. [Transferable] — [Skullgirls blog](https://skullgirls.com/2011/05/animation-getting-the-details-right/)
- Roblox forum version: "a couple of frames of very emphasised windup," then overextend before the hitbox fires. [Anecdotal] — [DevForum: Combat Animation Lacks Power](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/combat-animation-lacks-power/1875328)
- Moon Animator beginner guidance: add anticipation before major actions and slow it down (for example before a jump). [Anecdotal guide] — [ExitLag: Moon Animator guide](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/moon-animator-roblox/)
- Fast moves: "animating in 3s is mandatory for fast movements" (keys about 3 frames apart rather than 5). [Anecdotal, single commenter] — [YouTube: [OLD] How to animate R6 SMOOTHLY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0vjAiAR-YFk) (comment surfaced via search)

### Inferences
- For PvP M1s, keep anticipation tiny but *extreme* (1–3 frames at 60 fps, a big contrasting pull-back pose) and put telegraphing for heavy moves and ultimates into longer, clearly posed windups, VFX charge-ups and sound.
- Responsiveness tricks that preserve readability: the hitbox/marker fires on the strike frame, the recovery can be cancelled into the next M1 (chain), and follow-through carries the motion read after damage.

### Gaps
- No Roblox-specific numbers found for minimum readable startup under typical Roblox latency (ping adds roughly 50–150 ms, but no source tied this to animation startup).

## 4. Emotes and dances: syncing to BPM, building loops, keeping weight and rhythm

### Takeaway
No Roblox-specific BPM guide surfaced. Transferable practice: put **loop points on strong beats**, **match velocity (not just pose) across the loop seam**, make emote loops about **2–8 s**, and compute the beat interval exactly, because small timing errors drift out of sync over a few seconds.

### Cited Findings
- Loop points that coincide with a strong beat feel natural. "Match velocity, not just position" at the loop cut, or the restart hitches. A loop that works at 60 fps may pop at 30 fps, so test at the target rate. Successful dance emotes run about 2–8 s. Capture a long take and cut a 4–8 s loop. [Transferable, mocap vendor blog] — [MocapOnline: Dance Animation for Games](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/dance-animation-games-guide)
- Measure the beat's frame interval precisely. "Without an exact rhythm, the animation will look out of sync after some seconds." Test candidate intervals against the audio until markers line up. [Transferable] — [Animator Island: Animating to Music](https://www.animatorisland.com/animating-to-music/)
- Syncing emotes across players in-game is a scripting problem (time position / attributes), not an animation one. — [DevForum: Automatic Animation Sync](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/automatic-animation-sync/1675434)
- Whether R6 avatars can use Marketplace emotes: search results conflicted and came from low-quality sites. Not established. — [noping.com R15 avatar guide](https://noping.com/blog/roblox-r15-avatar-guide)

### Inferences
- Frames per beat = fps × 60 / BPM. At 60 fps and 120 BPM a beat is 30 frames. At 128 BPM it is 28.125 frames, which shows why non-integer intervals drift unless keys sit at exact times. Put the "hit" poses (down-bounce, contact) on the beat, with the torso/root dip landing on or just before it.
- For R6, the rhythm read mostly comes from torso and root bounce plus arm swings, since there are no knees to bend. Use vertical root/torso translation on the beat in place of knee flexion.

### Gaps
- No R6-specific emote/dance tutorial, no official Roblox emote-length guidance, and no confirmation of R6 vs R15 compatibility for Marketplace emotes were found.

## 5. Easing and timing: which easing styles to use and when, 60 vs 30 fps, keyframe density, holds and moving holds

### Takeaway
Roblox's Animation Editor offers per-key **Linear (default), Constant, Cubic/CubicV2, Elastic and Bounce**. Moon Animator offers more, including "None" (stepped) and Back. Community consensus: **don't leave everything Linear** (it reads robotic). Use **Cubic for organic, smooth motion**, **Constant/None for snaps and stepped anime/retro looks**, and treat Elastic/Bounce as stylized-only. Back/Elastic are sometimes recommended for punchy strikes. Many animators work at **60 fps** (Moon Animator 2 exports at 60). 30 fps can look floatier in-engine. Key only the major poses rather than every frame.

### Cited Findings
- Official definitions: **Constant** "removes interpolation between the selected keyframe and next keyframe, so the animation snaps from keyframe to keyframe." **CubicV2** eases with cubic interpolation, and Roblox's example uses it on a head turn because it "makes the animation of the character's motion appear more natural." **Elastic** moves as if on a rubber band. **Bounce** moves as if the start/end is bouncy. In the Curve Editor, Bounce/Elastic are not available as standard interpolations. [Official] — [Roblox Creator Docs: Animation Editor](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/editor); [Roblox Creator Docs: Curve Editor](https://create.roblox.com/docs/animation/curve-editor)
- Original staff announcement: easing styles Linear (default), Cubic, Constant, Elastic, Bounce. "Constant is an instant snap. You can tell it to instantly snap at the beginning, middle (inout) or end of the interpolation." (2016) [Official] — [DevForum: Animation Easing Styles](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animation-easing-styles/25374)
- "Don't do a full animation of linear keyframes if you haven't mastered easing styles first." Linear without In/Out easing "will look like a robot did it." [Widely repeated] — [Medium: Animating in Roblox](https://medium.com/@jesus.lo.be.30/animating-at-roblox-e9483e8d5dd6); [DevForum: Jespone's guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752)
- Cubic is "usually the smoothest for natural motion." Constant suits mechanical motion and hit reactions. Bounce suits landing impacts. EasingOut on attacks gives fast-start strikes, and EasingIn on wind-ups builds momentum. [Commercial guides, the exact page for each claim not pinned down] — [Kitsblox: Why Your Roblox Animations Look Choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations); [Kitsblox: How to Animate in Roblox Studio](https://kitsblox.com/blog/how-to-animate-in-roblox-studio); [Mocapgen: How to Use the Roblox Animation Editor (2026)](https://mocapgen.com/blog/how-to-use-roblox-animation-editor)
- Back and Elastic recommended as "more powerful" easing for combat strikes. [Anecdotal] — [DevForum: Combat Animation Lacks Power](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/combat-animation-lacks-power/1875328)
- **Frame rate:** Moon Animator 2 exports at 60 fps. A "None" easing preserves stepped low-FPS spacing on export. Roblox's editor was once fixed at 30 fps, and its custom-framerate setting changes only how frame numbers convert to seconds, not playback. A Blender user found 30 fps animations "looked floatier in Studio" while 60 fps matched better but doubled the keying work. One suggestion: animate at 30 and scale keys by 2 for a 60 fps export. [Mixed: official announcement plus anecdotal] — [DevForum: Moon animator FPS export only exporting 60 fps](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/moon-animator-fps-export-only-exporting-60-fps-animations/2514524); [DevForum: Set Custom Framerate in Animation Editor Beta](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-set-custom-framerate-in-animation-editor-beta/392218); [DevForum: Do you animate in 30FPS or in 60 FPS? (Blender)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/do-you-animate-in-30fps-or-in-60-fps-blender/3193170); [DevForum: Frame animation function built around Moon Animator 2](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/frame-animation-function-built-around-moon-animator-2/4214289)
- **Keyframe density:** too many keyframes cause stiffness because the engine has no room to interpolate. Key the major poses and let easing fill in. Fewer keyframes reduce jitter (twitchy legs). [Commercial blog plus anecdotal forum] — [Kitsblox: Why Your Roblox Animations Look Choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations); [DevForum: Feedback on my R6 Animation (First Time)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-r6-animation-first-time/3192280)
- Spacing basics: keys closer together give slower motion, keys farther apart give faster. [Generic/low-quality source] — [grandavehousing.calpoly.edu guide (SEO page)](https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/news/create-animations-in-roblox-a)
- **Holds:** vary how long each frame is held rather than holding every frame equally (Cartwright). The Overwatch GDC summary says her talk covered overshooting and holding keys. [Transferable] — [Cartwright GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcchina14/presentations/833784_MarielCartwright_PowerfulAndEffective_EN.pdf); [Overwatch animation talk summary](https://evasokolova.wordpress.com/2016/04/03/gdc-overwatch-animation-talk-summary/)

### Inferences
- Practical easing map for an R6 skill: anticipation key → Cubic (or Sine/Quad in Moon) easing *into* the wind-up pose. Wind-up → strike: Constant, or a very short fast-out ease, so the strike snaps. Strike → overshoot → settle: one frame overshoot, then Cubic out. Recovery: slower Cubic back to stance. Mechanical props: Linear. Elastic/Bounce: only for cartoony secondary motion or landings, never as the default.
- A "moving hold" (keep a held pose drifting slightly so it doesn't die) is standard animation practice, but **no Roblox source in results discussed it by name**. Treat it as general craft, not Roblox-community-sourced.

### Gaps
- Roblox's Animation Editor easing *direction* semantics (In vs Out) may differ from TweenService conventions. I recall community reports of this but could not verify. Check before encoding direction rules.
- No source gave a Roblox-community consensus on 60 vs 30 fps for combat specifically. The evidence leans toward 60 fps timelines (Moon 2 default), with Constant/None used for a stepped look.

## 6. Pose design for blocky rigs: silhouette, line of action, contrapposto, asymmetry, weight

### Takeaway
Roblox-specific posing advice is thin. The applicable rules come from general animation pedagogy and Cartwright: **clear silhouettes in key poses**, a **strong line of action**, **hip/shoulder counter-rotation (contrapposto)** for weight, **asymmetry (avoid twinning)**, and, for thin blocky limbs, **avoid busy overlapping limbs that clutter the silhouette**.

### Cited Findings
- A symmetrical body with no weight shift and a spine parallel to the screen edge looks lifeless. A small tilt and a clear line of action fix it. Hip/shoulder counter-rotation (contrapposto) gives natural weight shift. Thin limbs (as on blocky rigs) can create busy overlaps and criss-crossing lines, so keep the focal point clear and simplify. Beginners should use clean silhouettes and strong lines of action and avoid complex contact-heavy poses. [Transferable] — [Animation Mentor: Building Appealing Character Poses](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-building-appealing-character-poses-for-animation/)
- Clear silhouettes in key poses are the basis of readable fighting-game animation. [Transferable] — [Cartwright GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcchina14/presentations/833784_MarielCartwright_PowerfulAndEffective_EN.pdf)
- Line of action defined for Roblox posing as the imagined line through spine and limbs that suggests movement. [Low-authority guide] — [Oreate AI: Top-Tier Roblox Poses](https://create.oreateai.com/create/how-to-create-and-use-toptier-roblox-poses-for-your-avatars-and-gfx)
- Twinning (see section 7) is hidden with posing when timing matters. [Anecdotal] — [DevForum: Jespone's guide #12](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752/12)

### Inferences
- On R6, "contrapposto" is achieved by tilting/twisting the torso via RootJoint one way while counter-rotating the hips (Left/Right Hip motors) and offsetting the arms the other way. That is the only way to get hip-vs-shoulder opposition, since there is no waist joint.
- Combat stances should read from the default third-person over-the-shoulder camera, which sees the character mostly from behind and slightly above. Silhouette checks should be done from that angle, not only the front orthographic view.

### Gaps
- No Roblox/R6-specific posing tutorial (for example "R6 fighting stance angles") was found.

## 7. Most common beginner mistakes in R6 animation and how to fix them

### Takeaway
The most repeated beginner faults: **all-Linear easing (robotic)**, **too many keys / keying every frame (stiff, jittery)**, **twinning (symmetry and identical timing)**, **no torso bob or body motion (stiff)**, **weak recovery (punches read as taps)**, **legs too wide or raised too high in walks**, and **technical problems** (floating rigs, torso not animating, priority overrides).

### Cited Findings
- **All Linear** → robotic. Fix: learn easing and use Cubic/eases. [Widely repeated] — [Medium: Animating in Roblox](https://medium.com/@jesus.lo.be.30/animating-at-roblox-e9483e8d5dd6); [DevForum: Tips for making r6 animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tips-for-making-r6-animations/3679871)
- **Twinning:** one community definition is keys "in a straight line without inbetweens" (passing keyframes). Fix: block the important poses, then add in-betweens, and offset when parts move. Commenters admitted twinning in every animation they'd made. [Widely referenced] — [Medium: Animating in Roblox](https://medium.com/@jesus.lo.be.30/animating-at-roblox-e9483e8d5dd6); [DevForum: Jespone's guide #11](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752/11); [DevForum: Jespone's guide #12](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752/12). *Note: the standard animation definition of twinning is symmetrical poses or limbs moving in identical timing. The Medium author's definition is looser.*
- **Too many keys** → stiffness and jitter. Fix: key major poses, use fewer keyframes. [Commercial plus anecdotal] — [Kitsblox: Why Your Roblox Animations Look Choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations); [DevForum: Feedback on my R6 Animation (First Time)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-r6-animation-first-time/3192280)
- **Stiff walks:** add torso bob, ease the keys. Keep legs under the body. Don't lift legs above the waist. Don't move the same-side arm and leg together. — [DevForum: Tips for making r6 animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tips-for-making-r6-animations/3679871); [DevForum: Feedback on 2nd R6 Walk Animation](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-2nd-r6-walk-animation/1779706); [DevForum: Alternative R6 Animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/alternative-r6-animations/1684667)
- **Weak combat:** flat, static strikes with no windup, overextension or effortful recovery. Fix per section 2. — [DevForum: Combat Animation Lacks Power](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/combat-animation-lacks-power/1875328)
- **Limb pops in transitions:** "snapping limbs is something that the eye catches quite easily, but can also be remedied fairly quickly." — [itch.io jam review](https://itch.io/jam/sfasx-game-animation/rate/512055)
- **Floating rig:** a zombie appeared to float. The fix was moving the lower torso closer to the grid. Also check Humanoid HipHeight. — [DevForum: R6 floating animation problem](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-floating-animaition-problem/2540686)
- **Torso won't animate on R6:** caused by the Studio Beta "Adaptive Animations" ("I turned the beta feature off, and this issue was fixed," Dec 2025), or by PrimaryPart set wrong. Also raise animation priority. Reported more often with Moon Animator. — [DevForum: Torso will not move or rotate on R6 rigs (bug report)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/torso-will-not-move-or-rotate-on-r6-rigs/4148296); [DevForum: R6 Torso not moving or rotating in animation](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-torso-not-moving-or-rotating-in-animation/4147390); [DevForum: I Can't Animate R6 Torso](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/i-cant-animate-r6-torso/4220245)
- **Default Animate script overriding the torso:** a right-arm-only tool animation still moved the torso, traced to the default Animate script's "toolnone." Also, if two animations share a priority, one can overwrite joints the other doesn't animate. Set priorities correctly (Action for attacks). — [DevForum: R6 Animation overwrites Torso even when excluded](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-animation-overwrites-torso-even-when-excluded/4640830); [DevForum: Animations overlapping with each other](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animations-overlapping-with-eachother/2030897)
- **Foot sliding:** no Roblox-specific guidance found. The only result (Source Filmmaker, 2013) suggests building forward motion into the animation rather than fixing it after. [Weak] — [Steam: SFM discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1840/discussions/0/864959336695785863)

### Inferences
- Arms clipping through the torso is a common R6 issue because arms are rigid blocks that rotate at the shoulder. Fix it by swinging arms outward (adding a Z-axis "abduction" rotation) during cross-body motions or by twisting the torso to clear space. No source discussed this explicitly.
- Dash/move animations that bake root translation will look like sliding or desync with the scripted HRP velocity. Match the scripted displacement to the pose timing (section 2, root motion).

### Gaps
- No sources found on "poses that don't read from the camera's typical angle" or on arm-through-torso clipping for R6 specifically.

## 8. Workflow tips: blocking key poses first, pose-to-pose vs straight-ahead, testing in-game, mirroring, reference

### Takeaway
The repeated workflow is **pose-to-pose**: block the key poses (anticipation, wind-up, contact, follow-through, recovery), then add breakdowns/in-betweens, then easing and polish. Use **reference footage**, **test in-game** with real priority, markers and blends, and **chain combat moves through a shared stance**.

### Cited Findings
- Plan essential poses first, then fill in poses leading from one to the next. The guide covers straight-ahead vs pose-to-pose and secondary action. [Widely referenced] — [DevForum: Jespone's guide to animations](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752)
- "Do the important poses and then between them more things." [Anecdotal] — [Medium: Animating in Roblox](https://medium.com/@jesus.lo.be.30/animating-at-roblox-e9483e8d5dd6)
- Key the 5 major poses, then let easing fill. [Commercial] — [Kitsblox: Why Your Roblox Animations Look Choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)
- Use reference video of real people performing the action. Preview with the Animation Editor playback controls. [Generic] — [grandavehousing.calpoly.edu guide (SEO page)](https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/news/create-animations-in-roblox-a)
- Roblox's Animation Editor "smoothly animates the rig from pose-to-pose." [Official] — [Roblox creator-docs: animation index](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/animation/index.md)
- Publish with the correct priority and save the IDs for scripts (combat tutorial). — [SozAI transcript: How To Make A BATTLEGROUNDS GAME In Roblox Studio 2024](https://sozai.app/transcript/make-battlegrounds-game-roblox-studio/index.md)
- Tools: Moon Animator 2 (60 fps export, "None" easing, camera keyframing, wiggle fill), a Blender R6 IK/FK rig for animating outside Studio, and a free browser R6/R15 flipbook tool that can trace poses from video. — [DevForum: R6 IK + FK Blender Rig](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-ik-fk-blender-rig-v21/3586405); [GitHub: r6-flipbook](https://github.com/animeyeeter123/r6-flipbook)
- Anchor the dummy before posing so physics doesn't reset the pose. [Low-authority] — [Oreate AI: Roblox Poses](https://create.oreateai.com/create/how-to-create-and-use-toptier-roblox-poses-for-your-avatars-and-gfx)

### Inferences
- For a combat set, author the shared fighting stance (idle) first, then build every M1 to start and end on it so chains blend in about 0.1 s without pops.
- Mirroring: R6 is left/right symmetric, so mirrored poses (for example alternating M1 arms) are cheap. But a direct mirror plus identical timing creates twinning, so offset timing or pose details after mirroring. No source discussed mirroring explicitly.
