# Six Rigid Joints Reward Exact Math, Exaggeration

Good keyframed R6 animation comes from learning one small, strict system exactly and then acting beyond its limits. The rig is **seven parts joined by six Motor6Ds** (RootJoint, Neck, two Shoulders, two Hips). Every Pose you key becomes a **Motor6D.Transform in that joint's own rotated frame**. On shoulders and hips, a Z rotation swings the limb forward or back, with opposite signs on the left and right. On the neck and torso, X points to the character's left, Y points backward and Z points up. The traps in the file format and runtime are mechanical, not artistic. **Legacy Cubic easing plays reversed in-game, so use CubicV2.** **Roblox's default animations run at Core priority**, so custom clips must sit at Idle, Movement or Action, and clips at the same priority blend in proportion to weight instead of overriding each other. The stock R6 `Animate` script **speed-scales only its own walk ID and never plays "run"**. Tracks replicate only through an **Animator created on the server**. Since **July 31, 2025**, animations are Restricted assets that must be explicitly granted to each experience. R6 has no elbows, knees or waist, so weight, squash and reach come from the RootJoint (root bob, plus lean and twist paired with hip counter-rotation), from exaggerated poses and from snappy timing. For a 2-stud leg, Roblox's default 16 studs/s is physically a run, not a walk. Battlegrounds combat has standardised on a **four-hit M1 string**. Each hit has a short, extreme windup and a near-instant strike that overextends. It stops abruptly on a marker-driven contact frame with 2–4 frames of hit-stop, then an effortful recovery returns to a shared stance. Confidence varies. Engine facts rest on official docs and Roblox's own code. Runtime easing curves rest on one community measurement. Most craft advice comes from forum search excerpts and from fighting-game practice that transfers. No source gave concrete M1 frame budgets.

## Seven blocks and six motors fix every R6 pose

A default R6 character is **seven BaseParts**. The `HumanoidRootPart` (HRP) is invisible and non-colliding, and it shares one CFrame with the `Torso`; both are 2×2×1 studs. The `Head` is 2×1×1 and carries a Head-type SpecialMesh at 1.25 scale. The four limbs are 1×2×1, and their names contain spaces. Four independent rig dumps agree on every size and joint value, and one of them is a Roblox-authored 2018 avatar-editor model ([Adonis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Epix-Incorporated/Adonis/HEAD/MainModule/Server/Dependencies/Assets/RigR6.rbxmx), [RigConverter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RadiatedExodus/RigConverter/HEAD/src/RigConverter/R6_Rig.rbxmx), [Fleasion](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fleasion/Fleasion/HEAD/src/fleasion/cache/tools/animpreview/R6RIG.rbxmx), [Morpher2018](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/suscersal/roblox-studio-web/HEAD/content/avatar/morpherEditorR6.rbxmx)). Standing at the origin facing −Z, with +X as the character's right, the soles sit at y=0, the hip line at 2, the HRP and Torso centre at 3, the shoulder pivots at 3.5, the neck pivot at 4 and the Head centre at 4.5 ([Fleasion](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fleasion/Fleasion/HEAD/src/fleasion/cache/tools/animpreview/R6RIG.rbxmx)). That matches the official R6 rule **Height = LeftLeg.Size.Y + 0.5·RootPart.Size.Y + HipHeight = 2 + 1 + 0**, because default R6 HipHeight is 0 [Official] ([Humanoid](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Humanoid.yaml)). The same page says the Humanoid dies at once if the Head is not attached to a part named `Torso`. It also says R6 sets `PrimaryPart` to the Head, not the HRP, which has not been verified on live spawns [Official].

| Part | Size | Rest centre relative to Torso | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HumanoidRootPart` | 2×2×1 | (0, 0, 0) | Transparent, CanCollide off |
| `Torso` | 2×2×1 | (0, 0, 0) | Parent of five of the six joints |
| `Head` | 2×1×1 | (0, 1.5, 0) | Head SpecialMesh, scale 1.25 |
| `Right Arm` / `Left Arm` | 1×2×1 | (±1.5, 0, 0) | CanCollide off |
| `Right Leg` / `Left Leg` | 1×2×1 | (±0.5, −2, 0) | CanCollide off |

Six Motor6Ds connect the parts. `RootJoint` lives in the HRP and drives the Torso. `Neck`, `Right Shoulder`, `Left Shoulder`, `Right Hip` and `Left Hip` live in the Torso. Within each joint, C0 and C1 share one rotation, so an identity Transform leaves the part at its rest pose. The values below agree across all four dumps. They also match Roblox's own avatar-unification code, which comments that "orientation of r6 joints are setup slightly differently from r15 motors" ([AdaptInstance.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/avatar/unification/AdaptInstance.lua), [Adonis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Epix-Incorporated/Adonis/HEAD/MainModule/Server/Dependencies/Assets/RigR6.rbxmx)). In the table, `(x,y,z)·` means `CFrame.new(x,y,z) *`.

| Motor6D | Part0 → Part1 | C0 | C1 | Pivot location [D] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RootJoint` | HRP → Torso | `Angles(-π/2, 0, π)` | same as C0 | Torso centre |
| `Neck` | Torso → Head | `(0,1,0)·Angles(-π/2,0,π)` | `(0,-0.5,0)·Angles(-π/2,0,π)` | Top centre of Torso |
| `Right Shoulder` | Torso → Right Arm | `(1,0.5,0)·Angles(0,π/2,0)` | `(-0.5,0.5,0)·Angles(0,π/2,0)` | Arm's inner face, 0.5 below its top |
| `Left Shoulder` | Torso → Left Arm | `(-1,0.5,0)·Angles(0,-π/2,0)` | `(0.5,0.5,0)·Angles(0,-π/2,0)` | Mirror of right |
| `Right Hip` | Torso → Right Leg | `(1,-1,0)·Angles(0,π/2,0)` | `(0.5,1,0)·Angles(0,π/2,0)` | Leg's outer top edge |
| `Left Hip` | Torso → Left Leg | `(-1,-1,0)·Angles(0,-π/2,0)` | `(-0.5,1,0)·Angles(0,-π/2,0)` | Mirror of right |

One naming wrinkle: Studio's AssetImporter R6 rig builder calls the root motor "Root Hip", not `RootJoint` ([R6.luac.s](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AssetImporter/Src/Utility/RigSetup/R6.luac.s)). This rarely matters for playback, because **the animation system matches a pose channel to a joint by part names**. It reads them through `Motor6D.ChildName` and `ParentName`, "so a rig's part names must line up with the names authored in its animations" [Official] ([Motor6D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Motor6D.yaml)). The joint name does matter to any script that looks joints up by name. Part-name matching also explains the most common R6 failure. R15 clips key parts like `UpperTorso` and `LeftUpperArm`, find no matching R6 parts, and move nothing.

### Every key is a Transform in a rotated joint frame

The working relation is **Part1.CFrame = Part0.CFrame · C0 · Transform · C1⁻¹**. Officially, a weld maintains `part1.CFrame * C1 == Part0.CFrame * C0` [Official] ([Weld](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Weld.yaml)). The animation system "applies Pose.CFrame to the corresponding Motor6D by manipulating the relative transformation of the motor, the Motor6D.Transform property. The original C0 and C1 values are not changed" [Official] ([Pose](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Pose.yaml)). No official page prints the full formula with Transform in the middle. However, Roblox's own thumbnail code (CorePackages EmoteUtility, decompiled) applies keyframes exactly that way, and appends `CFrame.Angles(0,0,CurrentAngle)` for legacy Motor angles ([EmoteUtility](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EliCauvert/Roblox-Datamining/HEAD/InExperience/PatchRoot/DataModelInstances/CorePackages/Packages/_Index/Thumbnailing/Thumbnailing/EmoteUtility.luau)). A DevForum answer states the same equation (search excerpt) ([DevForum t/2415565](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/2415565)).

For an animator, this means **a Pose's rotation is expressed in its joint's C0 frame**, not in Torso or world space, and on R6 those frames are oddly oriented. The R6 pose tree is `HumanoidRootPart > Torso > {Head, Right Arm, Left Arm, Right Leg, Left Leg}`. The `Torso` pose drives RootJoint, `Head` drives Neck, and each limb pose drives its shoulder or hip. The HRP pose has no motor above it, so its CFrame does nothing. This is inferred from the official parent-pose rule and confirmed by the nesting in a real Studio-saved R6 walk ([Pose](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Pose.yaml), [R6 anim_walk.rbxmx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/workspacePWNAGE/Reboot2016/HEAD/corescript-patches/content/pwnsCorescriptPatches/rbxmx/anim_walk.rbxmx)).

Shoulder and hip frames are rotated ±90° about Y. Their joint +X points forward on the right side and backward on the left, +Y runs up the limb, and +Z points outward. Neck and RootJoint share the root rotation, which gives **+X = character left, +Y = backward, +Z = up**. Nothing official states these axes in words. They are derived from the exact default CFrames [D] and backed by three pieces of behavioural evidence. First, Roblox's legacy R6 Animate script sits the character with `RightShoulder:SetDesiredAngle(π/2)` and `LeftShoulder:SetDesiredAngle(−π/2)`, which puts all four limbs forward. It jumps with ±π, which puts the arms overhead. It walks by feeding the same sine to both shoulders and its negative to both hips, and that only swings the arms opposite the legs if the left side's sign is mirrored ([Animate2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/gear/HEAD/src/gear/85879465/src/LocalScript/Animate2.client.luau)). Those legacy angles rotate about joint Z ([EmoteUtility](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EliCauvert/Roblox-Datamining/HEAD/InExperience/PatchRoot/DataModelInstances/CorePackages/Packages/_Index/Thumbnailing/Thumbnailing/EmoteUtility.luau)). Second, the widely copied R6 head-follow snippet yaws the Neck about Z and pitches it with a negated sign, the reverse of its R15 branch ([head-follow snippet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CarlDV/InfiniteYieldPluginStore/HEAD/plugins/1301938690775580746/mfly.lua)). Third, the `Right Arm` pose in a classic R6 jump file is a pure +10° Z rotation ([roform fixture](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unreal-Works/roform/HEAD/fixtures/ANIMATION%20-%20Jump.rbxmx)). The resulting sign map uses θ > 0, and "+Z" means `CFrame.Angles(0,0,θ)`.

| Pose name → joint | Forward / back | Sideways | Twist / turn | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Right Arm` → Right Shoulder | +Z swings forward (π/2 horizontal, π overhead) | −X raises outward; +X crosses chest | +Y turns front face inward | — |
| `Left Arm` → Left Shoulder | **−Z** swings forward | −X raises outward; +X crosses chest | +Y turns front face outward | — |
| `Right Leg` → Right Hip | +Z kicks forward | −X out to side | +Y toes in | — |
| `Left Leg` → Left Hip | **−Z** kicks forward | −X out to side | +Y toes out | — |
| `Head` → Neck | +X nods down | +Y tilts toward left shoulder | +Z turns left | — |
| `Torso` → RootJoint | +X bows forward | +Y side-bends left | +Z twists left | `(0,0,−d)` lowers; `(0,−d,0)` moves forward; `(+d,0,0)` moves left |

Three rules fall out of the table [D]. First, **pitch (Z) and twist (Y) flip sign between the left and right limbs for the same anatomical motion, while abduction (X) does not**. Second, `CFrame.Angles(rx, ry, rz)` multiplies X·Y·Z, unlike `fromEulerAnglesYXZ`, and reading Euler angles back gimbal-locks near ±90° [Official] ([CFrame](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/datatypes/CFrame.yaml)). A tool that writes keys should therefore build limb rotations from single-axis factors in a fixed order: pitch, then abduction, then twist [R]. Third, the RootJoint translates in its own frame. **`CFrame.new(0, 0, −d)` lowers the torso, but `CFrame.new(0, −d, 0)` moves it forward**, a classic bug for animators coming from R15. It was not verified whether Studio's Animation Editor gizmo shows values in these same joint axes.

### Off-centre pivots make the torso drag the legs

The pivots are not on the limb centrelines [D]. The shoulder pivot sits on the arm's inner face, 0.5 studs below the arm's top. A 90° sideways raise therefore lifts the arm's top 0.5 studs above the Torso, and a twist orbits the arm around the Torso's edge instead of spinning it in place. The hip pivot sits on the leg's outer top edge, so abduction also shifts the leg. The bigger consequence is that **RootJoint rotates the Torso about its centre, one stud above the hips, and the legs hang off the Torso**. Any torso bow, lean or twist swings the legs with it, lifting or sliding the feet. Grounded poses therefore need compensating hip rotation. R6 developers describe exactly this fix: "changing the rotation Torso motor changes the rotation of the legs. To counteract this I have added an inverse rotation so it works" (search excerpt) ([DevForum 1945245](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/bending-player-torso-on-an-r6-rig/1945245)). Treat torso lean or twist plus inverse hip rotation as one operation (inference).

### Weapons, deaths and 2025–26 avatar changes leave R6 on Motor6Ds

When a Tool is equipped, a **Weld named `RightGrip`** joins `Right Arm` to the tool's `Handle`. Its C0 is `CFrame.new(0,−1,0)·Angles(−π/2,0,0)` and its C1 is `Tool.Grip`. No doc lists that C0, but Roblox's own gear scripts hard-code it ("from original right grip"). One of them replaces the weld with a Motor6D that uses the same C0 ([gear DeathEffect](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/gear/HEAD/src/gear/302502491/src/SwordScript/DeathEffect/Functions.luau), [MotorizeRightGrip](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/gear/HEAD/src/gear/127506257/src/EventsAndAnims/MotorizeRightGrip.server.luau)). The default grip is a weld and R6 has no wrist, so a sword's angle stays locked to the arm unless the rig gets an animatable joint for the handle (inference). The community R6 Blender rig supports weapon and accessory export through the Blender plugin for this kind of work (search excerpt) ([DevForum 3586405](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-ik-fk-blender-rig-v222/3586405)).

For deaths, `BreakJointsOnDeath` defaults to true, and removing the Neck kills the Humanoid unless `RequiresNeck` is false [Official] ([Humanoid](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Humanoid.yaml)). An authored death that should keep playing after the Dead state therefore needs its joints kept intact (inference). The Avatar Joint Upgrade, which swaps Motor6Ds for AnimationConstraints, applies to "R15 player characters" [Official] ([Motor6D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Motor6D.yaml)). Every `AvatarUnificationMode` value now carries the deprecation "Workspace.AvatarUnificationMode no longer has an effect; R6 avatars always load as R6 rigs" [Official] ([AvatarUnificationMode](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/enums/AvatarUnificationMode.yaml)). The Workspace page still describes an R6-to-R15 adapter, though ([Workspace API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Workspace)). Either way, R6 rigs keep Motor6Ds.

## A Pose is a joint-space Transform, and easing names can mislead

### The KeyframeSequence is a pose tree with Weight-0 placeholders

Studio stores an animation as a **KeyframeSequence**, which carries `Loop`, `Priority` and a hidden `AuthoredHipHeight`. It contains **Keyframes**, each with a `Time` in seconds. Each Keyframe contains **nested Poses** (`CFrame`, `EasingStyle`, `EasingDirection`, `Weight`) and optional **KeyframeMarker** children (`Name`, `Value`) [Official] ([KeyframeSequence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeSequence.yaml), [PoseBase](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/PoseBase.yaml), [KeyframeMarker](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeMarker.yaml)). Poses "are named in accordance with the BaseParts they correspond to and are structured in terms of joint hierarchy" [Official] ([Keyframe](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Keyframe.yaml)). The clip's length is the highest keyframe `Time` [Official].

Studio's own reflection defaults, captured by rbx-dom, are **`Loop = true`, `Priority = Action` and `AuthoredHipHeight = 2`** for the KeyframeSequence, and Linear easing, In direction and Weight 1 for Poses ([rbx-dom database](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rojo-rbx/rbx-dom/master/rbx_dom_lua/src/database.json)). The docs instead say AuthoredHipHeight defaults to 1.35 "since that is the hip height set for a standard R15 character" [Official] ([KeyframeSequence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeSequence.yaml)). Real R6 files carry 2, and the property's runtime effect on R6 is undocumented. Because the defaults are Loop on and Action priority, **set Loop and Priority explicitly on every clip** (inference).

`Pose.Weight` is officially deprecated [Official] ([PoseBase](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/PoseBase.yaml)), but it still works as a "keyed / not keyed" flag. Roblox's Animation Clip Editor (ACE), decompiled, writes a **Weight-0 placeholder Pose for every unkeyed ancestor**. For example, it writes a `Torso` placeholder when only `Right Arm` is keyed. It skips Weight-0 poses when loading, ignores the `HumanoidRootPart` pose unless an internal root-motion flag is on, and snaps key times to **2,400 ticks per second** ([RigSerialization](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AnimationClipEditor/Src/Util/RigSerialization.luac.s), [ACE Constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AnimationClipEditor/Src/Util/Constants.luac.s)). The runtime almost certainly treats Weight 0 the same way, since editor-made animations don't snap the torso to identity at those keys, but no doc says so (inference). In practice, sparse keyframes are fine, but a keyed limb needs its whole ancestor chain present. Write the HRP pose as Weight 0 with an identity CFrame, and never rely on fractional weights.

Two event mechanisms coexist. A **KeyframeMarker** fires `AnimationTrack:GetMarkerReachedSignal(name)` and passes its `Value` string as the argument. Duplicate markers each fire once [Official] ([KeyframeMarker](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeMarker.yaml)). A Keyframe with a non-default *name* fires the older `KeyframeReached`, which is "superseded by `AnimationTrack:GetMarkerReachedSignal()`" [Official] ([AnimationTrack API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/AnimationTrack)). The default Animate script still relies on keyframe names: **a clip that replaces a default character animation needs its final keyframe renamed `End`**, and the name is case-sensitive [Official] ([editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md)). Looping has its own catch. "A looping animation doesn't interpolate between the final keyframes and first keyframes", so duplicate the first pose as the last [Official] ([editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md)).

On disk, a KeyframeSequence is a standard XML model (`<roblox version="4">`). CFrames are `CoordinateFrame` elements holding 12 floats in X, Y, Z, R00…R22 order, and enums are numeric `token`s ([rbx-dom XML spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rojo-rbx/rbx-dom/master/docs/xml.md)). The trimmed excerpt below is modelled on a real Studio-saved R6 walk ([anim_walk.rbxmx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/workspacePWNAGE/Reboot2016/HEAD/corescript-patches/content/pwnsCorescriptPatches/rbxmx/anim_walk.rbxmx)) and shows the structure.

```xml
<roblox version="4">
  <Item class="KeyframeSequence" referent="RBX0">
    <Properties>
      <string name="Name">Walk</string>
      <bool name="Loop">true</bool>
      <token name="Priority">1</token>                 <!-- Movement -->
      <float name="AuthoredHipHeight">2</float>
    </Properties>
    <Item class="Keyframe" referent="RBX1">
      <Properties><float name="Time">0</float><string name="Name">Keyframe</string></Properties>
      <Item class="Pose" referent="RBX2">              <!-- root: Weight 0 placeholder -->
        <Properties>
          <string name="Name">HumanoidRootPart</string>
          <CoordinateFrame name="CFrame"><X>0</X><Y>0</Y><Z>0</Z><R00>1</R00><R01>0</R01><R02>0</R02>
            <R10>0</R10><R11>1</R11><R12>0</R12><R20>0</R20><R21>0</R21><R22>1</R22></CoordinateFrame>
          <token name="EasingStyle">0</token><token name="EasingDirection">0</token>
          <float name="Weight">0</float>
        </Properties>
        <!-- child Pose "Torso" (Weight 1, drives RootJoint), whose children are
             "Head", "Right Arm", "Left Arm", "Right Leg", "Left Leg" -->
      </Item>
      <Item class="KeyframeMarker" referent="RBX9">
        <Properties><string name="Name">Hit</string><string name="Value">heavy</string></Properties>
      </Item>
    </Item>
  </Item>
</roblox>
```

Rojo supports both `.rbxm` and `.rbxmx` [Official] ([Rojo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rojo-rbx/rojo.space/master/docs/sync-details.md)). So does the Open Cloud Assets API, which warns that files "edited outside of Roblox Studio might not upload or function" [Official] ([Open Cloud](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/cloud/guides/usage-assets.md)). Hand-generated XML is the practical choice for tooling. The minimal template in the research notes was never tested in Studio.

### CubicV2 means what its name says in-game, but legacy Cubic and Constant do not

Easing is set per Pose and governs the segment **from that key to the same joint's next key**. The styles are Linear (0), Constant (1), Elastic (2), Cubic (3, deprecated), Bounce (4) and CubicV2 (5). The directions are In (0), Out (1) and InOut (2) [Official] ([PoseEasingStyle](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/enums/PoseEasingStyle.yaml), [PoseEasingDirection](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/enums/PoseEasingDirection.yaml)). The official texts contradict each other. The direction enum says In and Out "are backwards from how these terms are used by most other animation tools and by TweenService". The editor guide says In is "slower at the beginning" and Out "faster at the beginning", which is the standard TweenService meaning [Official] ([PoseEasingDirection](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/enums/PoseEasingDirection.yaml), [editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md)).

The evidence resolves the conflict style by style. The decompiled ACE previews every style through `TweenService:GetValue` and maps directions by name ([KeyframeUtils](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AnimationClipEditor/Src/Util/KeyframeUtils.luac.s)). A long-running bug thread records that legacy Cubic "plays in reverse on a Humanoid" for In and Out, but not for InOut, Elastic or Bounce. It also records that in v646 Roblox added CubicV2, which "reverses the direction in the engine to match" the editor (search excerpt) ([DevForum 449068](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animation-cubic-easing-direction-reversed/449068)). An October 2026 community measurement used `Animator:StepAnimations`; it is one report, written with AI help, and was not re-run. It found CubicV2 In at 0.008/0.125/0.512 of the change at 20/50/80% of a segment, and Out at 0.488/0.875/0.992. These are exactly t³ and 1−(1−t)³, and legacy Cubic measured reversed ([issue #69](https://github.com/Cautioned/Blender-Animations-Plugin/issues/69)). The Blender plugin's exporter now maps Blender's ease-out to CubicV2 Out, "verified against Animator:StepAnimations" ([easing.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cautioned/Blender-Animations-Plugin/main/roblox_animations/animation/easing.py)).

| Style | `In` at runtime | `Out` at runtime | `InOut` at runtime | Matches editor/TweenService? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linear | linear | linear | linear | n/a |
| Constant | snaps to the next pose at segment start | **holds, then snaps at the next key** | snaps at midpoint | Editor preview always shows a hold |
| Elastic | wobble grows, ~−12.5% dip just before landing | ~+12.5% overshoot, then settles | elastic S-curve | Yes |
| Cubic (legacy) | acts as **ease-out** | acts as **ease-in** | normal | **No: reversed** |
| Bounce | bounces near the start, then lands | arrives and bounces into the pose | non-standard (both halves bounce-in) | In/Out yes; InOut no |
| CubicV2 | **slow start (t³)** | **fast start, decelerates into the next key** | standard S-curve | **Yes** |

Confidence is high for Cubic and CubicV2, medium-high for Elastic and Bounce In/Out, and medium for Constant and Bounce InOut. The practical rules are: **use CubicV2 for every eased key and never write legacy Cubic; use Constant + Out for stepped keys; avoid Bounce InOut, or bake it into dense keys**. One unresolved hazard deserves a play-test. The ACE's default direction is In. If it writes Constant + In when an animator changes only the style, the game would snap a segment early while the editor shows a hold. A 2016 staff post confirms that Constant's direction moves the snap to "the beginning, middle (inout) or end of the interpolation" [Official] ([DevForum 25374](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animation-easing-styles/25374)). The Curve Editor is a one-way door. Opening it converts the clip to a CurveAnimation, and the Quaternion-to-Euler step is irreversible. Bounce and Elastic get baked into extra keys, because curves support only Linear, Constant and Cubic [Official] ([Curve Editor](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/curve-editor.md)).

## Getting clips into Studio and live changed in 2025

### No built-in R6 IK pushes many animators to Moon Animator or Blender

Studio's built-in Animation Editor works on any Motor6D rig. It defaults to **30 fps**, with presets of 24, 30, 60 and 120 and custom rates from 1 to 120. It supports per-key easing, events, named keyframes, an Optimize Keyframes pass and the Curve Editor [Official] ([editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md), [ACE Constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AnimationClipEditor/Src/Util/Constants.luac.s)). Its IK is built for R15. The current constants name `ROBLOX_R15_IK_CONSTRAINTS` and hold only an R15 part map ([ACE Constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AnimationClipEditor/Src/Util/Constants.luac.s)), and IK was "just R15 for now" when it launched in 2018 (search excerpt) ([Release notes 355](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/release-notes-for-355/181348/10)). Treat IK on R6 as unavailable (inference). FBX import got an "Animation Import Improvements" update on 2026-08-04, but R6 support is undocumented (search excerpt) ([DevForum 4775593](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animation-import-improvements/4775593)). Onion skinning was still being requested in December 2025 (search excerpt) ([DevForum 4165825](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/update-on-the-default-animation-editor/4165825)).

Many R6 animators therefore use **Moon Animator 2**, a paid plugin that exports KeyframeSequences. It offers "None" (stepped) and Back easing plus keyframeable cameras, and it exports at 60 fps; the sources on it are weak ([nilo.io](https://nilo.io/articles/export-moon-animator-animation-roblox), [DevForum 2514524](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/moon-animator-fps-export-only-exporting-60-fps-animations/2514524)). Others use **Blender** with the GPL-3 "Blender Animations" plugin, which live-syncs over a local server (default port 31337) and transfers easing, together with a community R6 IK/FK rig ([plugin README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cautioned/Blender-Animations-Plugin/main/README.md), [DevForum 3586405](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-ik-fk-blender-rig-v222/3586405)). All three tools produce the same KeyframeSequence structure, so they interoperate. Frame rate is partly a matter of perception. The custom-framerate setting changes only how frame numbers convert to seconds (search excerpt) ([DevForum 392218](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-set-custom-framerate-in-animation-editor-beta/392218)). One Blender user found 30 fps animations "looked floatier in Studio", while 60 fps matched better at twice the keying work [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 3193170](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/do-you-animate-in-30fps-or-in-60-fps-blender/3193170)). Keep keys on whole frames of whichever rate you choose.

### AnimSaves is where the editor looks for clips

Saved animations live as KeyframeSequences under **`ServerStorage.RBX_ANIMSAVES.<RigName>`**, and the rig references them through an ObjectValue named `AnimSaves`. The decompiled editor also still reads legacy KeyframeSequences placed directly under the rig's `AnimSaves` [Official] ([editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md), [RigUtils](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/BuiltInPlugins/AnimationClipEditor/Src/Util/RigUtils.luac.s)). The official import path is to right-click **AnimSaves**, choose **Insert From File**, and then pick ⋯ > **Load** in the editor. If the rig has no AnimSaves yet, save any animation first, and make sure the file contains a KeyframeSequence rather than a lone Keyframe [Official] ([sharing animations](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/education/build-it-play-it-island-of-move/sharing-animations.md)). To test without publishing, `KeyframeSequenceProvider:RegisterKeyframeSequence(ks)` returns a temporary ID that "cannot be used outside of Studio" [Official] ([KeyframeSequenceProvider](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/KeyframeSequenceProvider.yaml)).

One recent R6 trap: when the Torso refused to move or rotate in the editor, the reported cause was the **"Adaptive Animations" Studio beta**, and turning it off fixed the problem (search excerpt, December 2025) ([DevForum 4148296](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/torso-will-not-move-or-rotate-on-r6-rigs/4148296), [DevForum 4220245](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/i-cant-animate-r6-torso/4220245)). The experience itself must be set to R6 in Avatar Settings. Inserting an R6 dummy does not change the game-wide rig (third-party guide) ([itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/?p=330010)).

### Since July 2025, every experience needs an explicit grant

To publish, use ⋯ > **Publish to Roblox**. If the animation will run in a group-owned experience, choose the group as Creator [Official] ([editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md)). The Open Cloud Assets API also accepts Animation uploads up to 20 MB through beta endpoints [Official] ([Open Cloud](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/cloud/guides/usage-assets.md)). The rules for who may *load* an animation changed on **July 31, 2025**. Animations are now **Restricted assets that can never be Open Use**. An owner grants access to specific experiences (by universe ID) and to collaborators from the Creator Dashboard's Permissions page. Once a game is granted access, "any player will be able to load it when playing the experience", and **a grant to a game cannot be revoked** [Official] ([asset privacy docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/projects/assets/privacy.md)) ([DevForum 3852101](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/improving-animation-asset-permissions/3852101), search excerpt). Before the change, the standard fix for "Failed to load animation with sanitized ID" was to re-upload under the experience's owner (search excerpt) ([DevForum 3111789](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/failed-to-load-animation-with-sanitized-id/3111789)). The rollout broke some group animations across experiences in the same group until staff manually set them to open use (search excerpt) ([DevForum 3852101 p4](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/improving-animation-asset-permissions/3852101?page=4)). It is still unresolved whether group-owned animations are now auto-granted to every experience in the group. The change also ended "spoofing", where developers re-uploaded other creators' animations as a workaround ([change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/remove-roblox-improving-animation-asset-permissions)).

## Core priority and server Animators decide what players see

### Load each clip once on a server-created Animator

Load through an **`Animator`** under the Humanoid or an AnimationController; `Humanoid:LoadAnimation` is deprecated. That Animator must be **created on the server** and must already be in Workspace when you call `LoadAnimation`, or the call errors [Official] ([Animator API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Animator), [Humanoid](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Humanoid.yaml)). `LoadAnimation` "always creates a **new** AnimationTrack", so load each clip once per Animator and reuse the track, or look it up with `GetTrackByAnimationId` [Official] ([Animator API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Animator)). Developers who skipped this hit an "Animation Track Limit of 256 tracks for one animator exceeded" error (search excerpt) ([DevForum 2622870](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tool-error-animation-track-limit-of-256-tracks-for-one-animator-exceeded/2622870)).

On the track, `Play(fadeTime = 0.1, weight = 1, speed = 1)`, `Stop(fadeTime = 0.1)`, `AdjustSpeed` and `AdjustWeight` do the work. **`Length` reads 0 until the asset loads**, and `TimePosition` can only be set after `Play()`. A runtime change to `Looped` takes effect after the current cycle. `Stopped` fires while the fade-out may still be moving the rig, and `Ended` fires once the rig is back to neutral [Official] ([AnimationTrack API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/AnimationTrack)). Preload by passing the Animation **instances** to `ContentProvider:PreloadAsync`, which avoids the hitch on first play [Official] ([ContentProvider](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ContentProvider)). This is the minimal runtime code needed to play a keyframed attack:

```lua
-- LocalScript on the player's own character (replicates through the server-created Animator)
local animator = character:WaitForChild("Humanoid"):WaitForChild("Animator")
local slash = animator:LoadAnimation(slashAnimation)   -- load once per Animator, then reuse
slash.Priority = Enum.AnimationPriority.Action          -- beats Core defaults on the joints it keys
slash.Looped = false
slash:GetMarkerReachedSignal("Hit"):Connect(function(param)
	-- open hitbox window, hit-stop, SFX/VFX, camera shake
end)
if not slash.IsPlaying then slash:Play(0.05) end         -- short fade keeps the start snappy
```

### Blending is per joint, and Core loses to everything

Seven priorities stack as **Action4 > Action3 > Action2 > Action > Movement > Idle > Core**, and "Roblox's default character animations, including catalog animation bundles, play at **Core** priority" [Official] ([AnimationPriority](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/AnimationPriority)). Blending works **per joint**. Tracks are evaluated from high to low priority until a joint's weight sum reaches 1.0. Tracks at the same priority "blend proportional to their track weights (normalized if the sum exceeds 1)" [Official] ([AnimationPriority](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/AnimationPriority)). That second rule came from the 2021–22 weighted-blend fix. Before it, a newer same-priority track at weight 1 overrode the older one, which Roblox called unintended (search excerpt) ([DevForum 1320674](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animation-engine-runtime-changes-and-fixes/1320674)). The `AnimationWeightedBlendFix` property has since vanished from the Workspace docs. The string `RbxLegacyAnimationBlending` and a "legacy blending analytics" flag remain in the current Studio binary, but you should author for weighted blending ([Workspace API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Workspace), [DeepStrings](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/DeepStrings.txt), [FVariables](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/FVariables.txt)).

Three consequences follow for animators (inference). A custom clip left at Core averages 50/50 with the default animations on every joint it keys, which is a common cause of "my animation looks weak". Two overlapping Action tracks split each shared joint evenly, so chain moves by stopping the previous track or by putting the newer one at Action2. Partial-body clips work only if you delete keys on the joints you don't want to drive. Any keyed joint counts as animated, even at its rest pose, so an upper-body R6 attack should carry no keys on the hips or RootJoint, nor on the Neck if the move doesn't need it.

### Only owner clients and the server replicate tracks

Tracks that a player's client plays on **its own character** replicate to the server and to everyone else. Any other rig, including NPCs and AnimationController props, "must be loaded and started on the server to replicate". And "if an Animator is created locally, then AnimationTracks loaded with that Animator will not replicate" [Official] ([Animator API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Animator)). `Motor6D.Transform` itself is NotReplicated [Official] ([Motor6D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Motor6D.yaml)). Play player actions from the owning client to avoid latency. Play NPC animation on the server, or deliberately play it client-side for large crowds and replicate only state (inference). Community NPC systems run 300 or more NPCs this way (search excerpt) ([Lightweight NPCs](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/lightweight-npcs/4224897)). Distant NPCs may look choppy by design. `Animator.PreferLodEnabled` (default true) lets the engine evaluate remote characters less often, and the docs advise disabling it only for important NPCs [Official] ([Animator API](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Animator)). One caveat for combat: no doc confirms that `GetMarkerReachedSignal` fires on the server or on other clients for a track the owner played. Authoritative hit detection should not hinge on a client marker alone (inference).

## Rigid limbs turn locomotion into geometry

### WalkSpeed 16 is a run for a 2-stud leg

Classical locomotion keys four poses per step: contact, down, passing and up. A natural walk is "on 12s" at 24 fps, or half a second per step [S] ([AnimSchool](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/03/14/walk-cycle-animation-tips/)). A tempo table adapted from Williams runs from a 16-frame cycle (fast cartoon walk or slow run) through 24 frames (brisk natural walk) and 32 (stroll) to 40 (tired or elderly) [S] ([Animation Explainers](https://animationexplainers.com/walk-cycle-animation/)). Within each step, keys fall on frames 1, 4, 7 and 10 [S] ([Spotlight FX](https://spotlightfx.com/blog/walk-cycle-animation-beginners-guide-to-creating-realistic-movement)). Arms swing opposite the legs, peak near contact and lag slightly behind the body [S] ([Island Class](https://islandclass.org/2026/09/26/walk-cycle-animation-for-games/), [Rosenstock](https://joshuarosenstock.com/teaching/GPH213/walkcycle/walk.html)). At contact, "hips rotate toward the front leg, chest opposes the hips, arms oppose the legs, head opposes the chest" (search excerpt, attribution unverified) ([Rusty Animator](https://rustyanimator.com/run-cycle/)). Roblox's editor defaults to 30 fps, so multiply 24-fps charts by 1.25, or work in seconds [D] ([editor guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/animation/editor.md)).

R6 breaks the classical model because **a rigid 2-stud leg cannot shorten**. For the planted foot to stay on the floor, the hip must drop at contact by **Δy = L(1 − cos θ)** for a leg swung ±θ. The rigid-leg bob is therefore lowest at contact and highest at passing, not at "down" and "up" [D]. Step length is **s = 2L·sin θ**. A foot stays slide-free only if the cycle period is **T = s / (β·v)**, where β is the fraction of the cycle each foot spends on the ground [D]. The table uses Roblox's default WalkSpeed of 16 studs/s [Official] ([Humanoid](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Humanoid.yaml)) and L = 2 studs [D].

| Leg swing ±θ | Step length | Root drop at contact | Slide-free cycle, symmetric (β = 0.5) | Slide-free cycle, run (β = 0.35) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20° | 1.37 studs | 0.12 studs | 0.17 s | 0.24 s |
| 30° | 2.00 | 0.27 | 0.25 | 0.36 |
| 40° | 2.57 | 0.47 | 0.32 | 0.46 |
| 45° | 2.83 | 0.59 | 0.35 | **0.51** |
| 60° | 3.46 | 1.00 | 0.43 | 0.62 |

Two conclusions follow. First, **WalkSpeed 16 is a run for R6 proportions**. A slide-free, symmetric ±30° cycle lasts only 0.25 s, which looks frantic. The Froude number, 16²/(196.2 × 2) ≈ 0.65, sits above the ~0.5 at which humans switch from walking to running [D] ([Workspace gravity](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Workspace.yaml), [walk–run transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_from_walking_to_running)). A natural 1-second walk cycle matches only about 2.3–4.7 studs/s [D]. So author a true walk for about 8 studs/s or less, and a jog or run with a flight phase for 16 [R]. Roblox's own legacy procedural run swung ±57° at 9 rad/s and covered only about 60% of ground speed. That is the classic slide players accepted [D] ([old-roblox-animation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cambam239053cam/old-roblox-animation/main/script)).

Second, the physically consistent default at 16 studs/s is a **run cycle of about 0.5 s**. That is 0.25 s per step, or "runs on 6s" at 24 fps. Put contacts at ±40–45°, keep each foot on the ground for about 35% of the cycle, include two short flight phases, and use a root bob of 0.3–0.6 studs, low at contact and high in flight [R]. Keeping a foot fixed while the hip moves at constant speed requires a stance-leg angle of φ(t) = asin(sin θ − v·t/L), which is nearly linear. So use **Linear easing from contact to toe-off**, and CubicV2 InOut on the swing leg and arms [D/R]. Store each clip's authored speed, v = 2L·sin θ / (β·T), and scale playback by the current speed divided by that value, clamped to roughly 0.5–1.5× [R]. The stock R6 Animate won't do this for you.

The upper body has to supply everything knees and a waist normally would [R]. Real walking raises the centre of mass 2.7–4.8 cm, about 0.10–0.17 studs at 28 cm per stud [S/D] ([PubMed 15685471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15685471/), [Roblox units](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/physics/units.md)). Blocky characters need about 0.1–0.25 studs in a walk and 0.3–0.6 in a run for the bob to read. Keying the root low over the first quarter of each step and high over the third quarter fakes knee absorption and push-off. Chest and pelvis are one block, so counter-rotation has to be replaced. Yaw the torso ±3–8° toward the forward arm, roll it ±2–4° toward the swinging leg at passing, and shift it sideways 0.05–0.15 studs over the stance leg. Lean it forward about 0–5° for a walk, 10–15° for a jog and 15–25° for a sprint, and always counter-rotate the hips. In a walk, swing the arms at 0.5–0.8× the leg amplitude. In a run, imply bent elbows with a +10–25° forward bias and a ±35–60° swing that travels further forward than back. Counter-rotate the Neck 50–100% against the torso and let it lag 1–2 frames at 30 fps.

R6 feedback threads converge on the same fixes: more torso bob, eased keys, legs kept under the body and never lifted above the waist, and never swinging the same-side arm and leg together [Widely repeated] ([DevForum 3679871](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tips-for-making-r6-animations/3679871), [DevForum 1684667](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/alternative-r6-animations/1684667), [DevForum 1779706](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-2nd-r6-walk-animation/1779706)). Roblox's own tutorial agrees that the rig shape dictates the gait: a blocky rig "with mass spread through the body and limited range of motion might shuffle with a short stride" [Official] ([Create character animations](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/animation/create-an-animation)).

### Jumps, idles and hits must fit Roblox's physics clock

Roblox applies the jump impulse the moment the player presses jump, so **there is no room for a classical pre-jump crouch**. At JumpPower 50 and gravity 196.2 studs/s², the character peaks at 6.37 studs after 0.255 s and lands about 0.51 s after takeoff. The top 10% of the height already takes about 0.16 s, so the physics provides the apex hang [D] ([Humanoid](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Humanoid.yaml), [Workspace](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Workspace.yaml)). The stock 0.3 s jump clip therefore spans roughly takeoff to apex, and the looping fall covers the rest [D].

The recommended set [R] works around this. Fake the anticipation with a 1–2-frame squash at the start of the jump clip: root −0.3 studs, arms back 20–30°, torso pitched 10°. Stretch through takeoff in the first 0.06 s, with the arms whipping up 120–170° on a fast-start Out ease. Tuck the lead leg 30–45° through the rise. Loop a quiet apex and fall pose for 0.6–1.0 s, with the arms up 100–150° and the legs split 15–25°. Finally, add a separate **landing clip triggered on the Humanoid's Landed state**, at Action priority. It compresses for 0.05–0.1 s, dropping the root 0.4–0.6 studs with the legs splayed 15–20° and the torso pitched 15–25°. It then recovers over 0.15–0.3 s on an Out ease with a small overshoot, and the player can cancel it after about 0.1 s. Game Anim's point that slower actions can still feel responsive "if part of the character visibly reacts to the command right away" justifies this compromise (search excerpt) ([Game Anim](https://dokumen.pub/game-anim-video-game-animation-explained-2nbsped-9781000357806-1000357805.html)).

Idles fail when they freeze or pop. A mocap vendor puts a simple breathing loop at 2–4 s and a weight-shift loop at 8–12 s. It sets breathing at 15–20 breaths a minute with only 1–2 cm of travel, and warns that a loop without visible breathing reads as "a frozen statue". It also says a single loop becomes noticeable after about 90 s, which is a single-source claim [S] ([MoCap Online](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/idle-animation-game-dev-guide)). For R6, that means a root and torso rise of 0.03–0.08 studs and a 1–2° chest lift on a 3–4 s breath [R]. Add root sway of ±0.05–0.15 studs and ±1–3° of torso roll on a 4–8 s period, with the hips counter-rolling so the feet stay planted [R]. Stagger the parts so the torso leads and the arms and head trail by 0.1–0.4 s [R]. Make the loop length a whole multiple of every component's period, and end on the starting pose. Use Linear rather than eased keys through the seam for channels that keep moving, because CubicV2 InOut on both end keys stalls at the loop point [R]. Roblox's own R6 idle uses a 9:1 weighted pair of variants [S] ([MechaXYZ copy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MechaXYZ/VSBScripts/main/ROBLOX%20Animation%20Script)). A sensible minimum is one 2–5 s "cycle-breaker" variant, weighted 1:5 to 1:10 against the base loop [R].

Hit reactions invert the jump: **snap in, recover slowly**. A game-dev guide gives 10–25 frames for a standard hit and two to three times that for a stagger. It warns that a 3–6-frame blend into the reaction "can make responses feel sluggish" (search excerpt, attribution unverified) ([Combat Recall](http://combatrecall.blogspot.com/2018/05/reaction-animations-reaction-animations.html)). An R6 template [R] snaps within 0.05 s (Constant or Linear) to a pose thrown away from the force. Pitch or roll the torso 10–25°, shift the root 0.2–0.5 studs, whip the head 1.3–1.5× further a frame late, and fling the arms 20–60°. Hold for 0.03–0.1 s through hit-stop. Then recover over 0.25–0.6 s with an Out ease and a 5–10% overshoot, cancellable at about 60%. Four directional variants cover most needs. For knockdowns and deaths, transferable practice is a short authored reaction followed by a 0.1–0.3 s blend into ragdoll [Transferable] ([MoCap Online ragdoll](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/ragdoll-physics-animation-guide)). Get-ups wait for the body to settle, then blend over 0.4–0.8 s ([raduacg](https://github.com/raduacg/game-mechanics-optimizations/blob/main/117_ragdoll_blending.md)). Game gravity makes physics collapses fast: a torso falling 3 studs lands in 0.175 s [D]. An authored death fall therefore reads better at 0.4–0.8 s [R].

### The twelve principles rebuilt for six blocks

CFrames carry no scale, so R6 parts cannot squash or stretch [D]. Every principle that depends on deformation has to be rebuilt from root motion, limb angles and timing. No published source adapts the principles to rigid rigs, so all the substitutes below are [R] unless marked.

| Principle | R6 substitute |
|---|---|
| Squash and stretch | Squash: root dip 0.2–0.6 studs, legs splayed ±10–20°, torso pitched, arms in. Stretch: root up, legs together, arms overhead. Optionally offset limbs 0.1–0.3 studs along their axis for 1–3 frames as a smear |
| Anticipation | A reverse move ⅓–½ the size of the action over 0.1–0.3 s. For moves triggered by player input, keep it ≤0.1 s or fold it into the first frames |
| Staging | Arms at least 15–20° clear of the torso; torso turned 10–30° toward the target |
| Follow-through and overlap | Torso leads; head lags 1–3 frames and arms 2–4 frames at 30 fps, each overshooting 5–15% |
| Arcs | Hands and feet already trace circles of radius 1.5 and 2 studs about fixed pivots [D]; add root bob and sway so the body path arcs too |
| Secondary action | Head look-arounds, breathing in the arms, tool sway, at ≤30% of the primary amplitude |
| Exaggeration | 1.3–1.5× realistic angles: legs ±40–60° in runs, arms up to ±60–90° |
| Solid posing and appeal | Break left–right symmetry by 5–15°, add torso roll or yaw, keep the centre of mass over the support foot |

Spacing does the rest. Favour keys by placing breakdowns a third or two-thirds of the way between extremes, rather than halfway [R]. For a move of size A, overshoot by 10–20% two to four frames after arrival, undershoot by 5–10%, and settle within 6–10 frames, halving each oscillation [D/R]. This follows the decaying-spring heuristic ([Sunstrike](https://sunstrikestudios.com/en/blog/timing_in_animation/)). Replace dead holds longer than about 0.2–0.3 s with moving holds that drift 2–5% further in the same direction [R]. This is the moving hold Animation Mentor describes, with the overshoot "taken up a notch" ([Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/why-all-animators-need-to-master-the-moving-hold/)). Jonathan Cooper's warning applies doubly to blocky rigs: "we're afraid to push timing far enough, leaving motions weak and floaty" ([Cooper on X](https://twitter.com/GameAnim/status/969611403584655360)).

## Battlegrounds combat runs on snapping poses and four-hit strings

Every community claim in this section was seen only through search excerpts. The reliability labels come from the research notes.

### The R6 look is limited animation

The R6 combat style is effectively limited animation, as in anime and fighting games: few, extreme key poses with very little in-between. R6 animators advise exaggeration because "for rigs with less joint than the amount of joints a human has, exaggerating the movement helps the character feel more alive" [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 1533251](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advice-for-positoning-r6-limbs-while-animating/1533251)). They describe the target as "snappy": "snappy animations typically have fast acceleration and deceleration", built from "fewer keyframes with exaggerated poses" [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 2419921](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/fist-retro-style-animation/2419921)). The popular low-FPS look sets keys to Constant (Moon Animator calls it None), on the principle that "the less key frames you add, the more blocky it will be". The caveat is that Constant keys don't step the blends *between* animations [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 2800541](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-low-fps-blocky-style-animation/2800541)). Given the runtime easing measurements, set those Constant keys to **Out** and check them in a play-test (inference).

Without elbows, reach and power cannot come from an arm straightening. A punch extends through **RootJoint yaw plus shoulder rotation plus a forward root translation**, with the hips counter-rotated (inference). Arcs remain the test of life. Jespone's long-running guide says that without arcs the character looks like a robot, and it cautions that overlap is easy to overdo on a rig [Widely repeated] ([Jespone's guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752)).

### A four-hit string of snap, stop and recover

The genre standard, set by The Strongest Battlegrounds, is a **four-hit M1 string**. Hits one to three apply about **0.2 s** of light hitstun that holds the target in place. The fourth hit delivers a heavy knockback or downslam that usually ragdolls the target, and a knocked-down player is out of action for about 2 s. These figures come from community wikis, not official sources ([games.gg](https://games.gg/roblox/guides/the-strongest-battlegrounds-combat-guide/), [TSB Wiki](https://the-strongest-battlegrounds-rblx.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Combat)). Developers typically add an uptilt and a downslam, and reset the combo on a timer [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 3101051](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/logic-gate-for-combat-system/3101051)). Commercial R6 packs ship M1s "chained, so they will flow better". They return every move to a shared fighting stance and put hit markers on every attack [Anecdotal] ([Gumroad](https://citrusapples.gumroad.com/l/RobloxCombatSet), [itch.io wollnie](https://wollnie.itch.io/combat-animation-pack-r6), [BuiltByBit](https://builtbybit.com/resources/r6-combat-animation-pack.129091/)). Scripters stress stopping the current M1 before starting the next so tracks don't blend into mush, which the same-priority blending rule explains [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 3639185](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-smoothly-transition-between-m1-animations/3639185)).

Sources agree on what makes a strike powerful. Replies in "Combat Animation Lacks Power", drawing on Mariel Cartwright's GDC talk, call for "a couple of frames of very emphasised windup", a strike that overextends then retracts, and skipped frames or smears. They also ask for a recovery that shows the character "has to recover from using great force". Without that recovery, the original punch read as "a light tap" [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 1875328](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/combat-animation-lacks-power/1875328)). Cartwright's own slides add that even one frame of anticipation adds contrast, that one frame can overshoot the final pose, and that holds should vary in length. They also warn against over-animation, citing a 21-frame knee-fall as too long [Transferable] ([Cartwright GDC](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcchina14/presentations/833784_MarielCartwright_PowerfulAndEffective_EN.pdf)). Skullgirls often snaps straight to the active pose and lets follow-through sell the motion, while staying cancellable [Transferable] ([Skullgirls](https://skullgirls.com/2011/05/animation-getting-the-details-right/)).

Impact is then layered onto the contact frame. Freeze both characters with **hit-stop of about 2–4 frames** on heavy hits [Anecdotal] ([Kitsblox combat](https://kitsblox.com/blog/how-to-make-combat-system-roblox)); a Godot write-up uses about 0.05 s for normal hits and 0.09 s for finishers [Transferable] ([dev.to](https://dev.to/hirodeath/improving-combat-feel-with-hit-stop-slash-effects-and-combo-animations-3dbo)). Hold impact frames for no longer than one frame at 24 fps, and save them for big hits [Anecdotal] ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-add-impact-frames-on-moon-animator)). Fire hitboxes, VFX, SFX, hit-stop and camera shake from an animation marker, so "the moment of damage should match the visible impact" [Widely repeated] ([DevForum 3244308](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-animationevents-for-combat-system/3244308)). A 0.1 s fade between attacks is short enough to hide the blend [Anecdotal] ([Kitsblox choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)).

Put together, a defensible M1 runs as follows (inference, since no source gave Roblox frame budgets). It starts with a 1–3-frame extreme pull-back at 60 fps. The strike snaps or spans 1–2 frames and overshoots by one. A marker sits on the contact frame, with hit-stop. A slower recovery lands on the shared stance. Alternate arms across hits one to three, and commit the whole body on the fourth: a full torso twist, a step-in or a jump. At 30 fps, 0.2 s of hitstun is six frames [D]. Player attacks need fast startup, while enemy and NPC attacks need longer anticipation so players can react [Transferable] ([Cartwright GDC](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdcchina14/presentations/833784_MarielCartwright_PowerfulAndEffective_EN.pdf)). Keep the telegraphing for heavy moves and ultimates in longer windups, VFX and sound (inference).

### Strike easing advice conflicts, partly because of the old Cubic bug

Easing advice for strikes conflicts, and the conflict is instructive. One forum answer says to start the movement "slowly, then ending it off fast" [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 1533251](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advice-for-positoning-r6-limbs-while-animating/1533251)). Another prescribes a fast swing, an abrupt stop and a slow retract [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 2042214](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-would-i-go-about-making-my-combat-feel-more-impactful/2042214)). Both describe a strike that accelerates into contact. Commercial guides instead say that easing Out on attacks gives fast-start strikes [Anecdotal] ([Kitsblox choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)), and the body-mechanics research recommends Out into the extreme [R]. Much of this advice predates CubicV2, when legacy Cubic Out played as ease-in in-game. Animators may therefore have tuned "Out" by eye while actually getting the opposite curve (inference).

This report takes the following position. Over a strike of 1–3 frames, the curve changes only one or two in-betweens. So key the strike Linear or Constant, or CubicV2 In to accelerate into contact, and never cushion the arrival. Put the deceleration after contact, as a one-frame overshoot followed by a CubicV2 Out settle. Reserve Elastic and Back-style overshoot for stylised strikes, and Bounce for landings [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 1875328](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/combat-animation-lacks-power/1875328), [Kitsblox choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)).

### Dashes need scripted root motion

Movement attacks need scripting because **Roblox has no built-in root motion**. Experienced developers say the HRP is not meant to be animated. The options are to script the dash (velocity or CFrame on the HRP) while the limb clip plays, to extract and strip root curves from the KeyframeSequence, or to use a community RootMotion module (search excerpt) ([DevForum 1556616](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-root-motion-animating-the-humanoidrootpart/1556616), [RootMotion module](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/rootmotion-module-animate-the-humanoidrootpart-with-ease-simple-no-conversion-needed/3843087)). This matches the editor ignoring the HRP pose when it loads a clip. Match the scripted displacement to the pose timing, or a baked lunge will slide (inference). Cinematic ultimates are commonly authored in Moon Animator with keyframed cameras, which are played back on RenderStepped (search excerpt) ([DevForum 2427795](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/moon-animator-2-camera-animation-scripting/2427795)).

### Emotes, beginner mistakes and workflow

For dances, put loop points on strong beats, match velocity as well as pose across the seam, and keep loops around 2–8 s [Transferable] ([MoCap Online dance](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/dance-animation-games-guide)). A beat lasts fps × 60 / BPM frames, so 128 BPM at 60 fps is 28.125 frames. Keys must sit at exact times, or the animation drifts off the beat [D] ([Animator Island](https://www.animatorisland.com/animating-to-music/)). On R6, the rhythm reads mostly from root and torso bounce on the beat, standing in for bent knees (inference).

The same beginner faults recur across threads. All-Linear keys look "like a robot did it". Too many keys leave no room to interpolate and cause jitter. Other common faults are twinning, no torso bob, weak recovery, and legs spread too wide or lifted too high [Widely repeated] ([Medium](https://medium.com/@jesus.lo.be.30/animating-at-roblox-e9483e8d5dd6), [Kitsblox choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations), [DevForum 3192280](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-r6-animation-first-time/3192280)). Some problems look like animation faults but are technical. A floating rig calls for checking HipHeight and the root offset [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 2540686](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-floating-animaition-problem/2540686)). In one case, an arm-only tool clip still moved the torso because the default Animate's `toolnone` was playing underneath, and correct priorities fixed it [Anecdotal] ([DevForum 4640830](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/r6-animation-overwrites-torso-even-when-excluded/4640830)). The workflow that avoids these faults is pose-to-pose. Block anticipation, wind-up, contact, follow-through and recovery, add breakdowns, then ease and polish [Widely repeated] ([Jespone's guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/jespones-guide-to-animations/225752), [Kitsblox choppy](https://kitsblox.com/blog/fix-choppy-roblox-animations)). For a combat set, author the shared stance first and build every move to start and end on it (inference). Mirror alternating attacks, then offset their timing to avoid twinning, and judge silhouettes from Roblox's default over-the-shoulder camera rather than an orthographic front view (inference).

## Conclusion

On R6, much of the craft moves from the limbs to the root. The RootJoint is the only joint that can provide squash (by dipping), contrapposto (by twisting against counter-rotated hips), reach (by translating forward) and the weight bob of a walk. Because the legs hang from it, every one of those moves needs matching hip compensation. The rig's geometry also conflicts with Roblox's gameplay defaults. With a 2-stud leg, 16 studs/s and gravity of 196.2 studs/s², you cannot animate a walk that doesn't slide or a crouch before the jump impulse. Handling that is a design decision, not a polish task. You can tune WalkSpeed to fit the animation, author runs and faked anticipation that fit the physics, or accept the stylised slide players tolerated for a decade.

The biggest quality risk lies in the gap between what the editor shows and what the game plays, not in artistry. Several mechanics are undocumented or documented inconsistently: easing names that reverse (legacy Cubic) or snap early (Constant), Core-priority clips that silently average 50/50, an Animate script that speed-scales only its own walk, Weight-0 placeholder semantics, and marker replication. The play-test is the ground truth, not the editor preview. A small `StepAnimations` test harness would settle most of the open questions. Those questions are concrete M1 frame budgets (no source gave them), easing curves that rest on one AI-assisted measurement, and a current R6 Animate source that could not be read. An engine flag also hints that R15 animations may soon retarget onto R6 rigs, which would overturn today's rule that R15 clips do nothing on R6.
